    The awkward questions

    Asked before you have to ask them.

    Is this going to cost me staff?

    Usually not, and I'd be wary of anyone promising it will. In a business your size the bottleneck isn't headcount, it's that your good people lose a chunk of every week to work a machine should be doing. The gain shows up as more done with the people you've got — which is what you need if you're growing and can't afford the next hire yet.

    We've been burned by an agency before.

    Most people have. That's why the audit is fixed-price and comes off the build, why there's no retainer until something is live and working, and why everything gets documented so you can take it in-house or to someone else. If I disappeared tomorrow your business would still run.

    Our data is a mess. Aren't we too early for this?

    Everyone's data is a mess. Mine was. You don't need a data warehouse to stop someone re-typing invoices into a spreadsheet. I start with the jobs that don't depend on clean data, and the data tends to tidy itself up as a side effect of automating the thing that kept breaking it.

    Cashflow is tight. That's the whole problem.

    Understood — it's why the first step is free and the second is £1,200 rather than a five-figure engagement. I'd rather find you one thing that pays for itself inside a quarter than sell you a programme. If the audit turns up nothing worth building, I'll tell you that on the day rather than write it up nicely and invoice you anyway.

    Do you actually build it, or just write a report?

    I build it. The audit includes something working before I leave, so you can see the difference between a slide deck and a thing that runs on Tuesday morning without anyone touching it.

    What if we're in the same trade as you?

    Then I'll turn you down. I won't take on a business that competes with mine, and I don't reuse one client's numbers or systems on another's account. Say so early and neither of us wastes an hour.
