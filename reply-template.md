# Reply template

For answering an audit submission from your phone. Adapt it — don't send it as-is,
that's the whole point of not building a drip sequence.

**Rules that make it work**

- Reply the same day if you can, next working day at the latest. Speed *is* the product.
- Name their top task and their number in the first two lines. Prove you read it.
- Give one specific, useful thing away for free. Something they could do this week without you.
- End with **one question**, not a pitch. A question they can answer in a sentence.
- Never mention the audit tool, the website, or the word "book". If they want the call
  they'll ask, and they'll ask sooner if you haven't asked first.
- Plain text. No signature block with a logo. No links except the calendar one, and only
  if they've asked for it.

---

## The standard one

> Subject: **Re: your figures — {top task}**
>
> {First name},
>
> Thanks for putting the numbers in. {Total} a year, with {top task} the biggest
> single line at {top task cost}, is about what I'd expect for {staff count / their trade}
> — and it's the sort of number that's easy to carry for years because it never shows
> up as a line on the P&L.
>
> Looking at the three that came out on top:
>
> **1. {Task one} — {cost}/yr.** {One sentence on what's actually going on, in their world.}
> {One sentence on how you'd approach it, naming the tools.} {If it's a fortnight's work, say so.}
>
> **2. {Task two} — {cost}/yr.** {Same shape. Be honest if this one's harder than it looks.}
>
> **3. {Task three} — {cost}/yr.** {Same shape. If it's not worth doing yet, say that instead.}
>
> One thing you could do this week without me: {specific, concrete, free}. {Half a
> sentence on what it'd save.} It won't fix it, but it'll tell you whether the number's
> real before you spend anything.
>
> {The question. One only. See the list below.}
>
> Graham
>
> —
> I run a retail and e-commerce business in Barry and automate the boring half of it.
> This is the same thing, for someone else's business.

---

## Questions that work as the closer

Pick one. Never two.

- "Which of those three would annoy you least to lose?"
- "Who actually does the {top task} at the moment — you, or someone you'd rather was doing something else?"
- "Is the {top task} number the one that surprised you, or was it the total?"
- "What's the system it's being re-typed *into*? That usually decides whether this is a fortnight or a month."
- "Has anyone tried to fix this before, and what went wrong?"

## Variants

**Zero-hours submission** (they answered "none" to everything). Rare, and usually
worth a reply anyway — they're curious, not a lead yet.

> You came out at nothing, which either means you're genuinely tidy or the hours are
> spread thin enough that nobody notices them. It's normally the second. If you want a
> sanity check, tell me what the last thing was that someone in the office grumbled
> about doing — that's usually where it's hiding.

**Very large number** (over about £60k). Resist the temptation to get excited.

> That's a big number, and I'd want to poke at it before either of us takes it at face
> value — a total that size usually means one job is being counted twice, or that a
> couple of people are doing the same thing in parallel. Worth twenty minutes to find
> out which.

**Same trade as you.** Send it fast and send it kindly.

> I've got to be straight with you before we go any further: {their trade} is what I do
> for a living, and I don't take on businesses that compete with mine. Not a judgement
> on yours — just how I keep the confidentiality promise honest.
>
> That said, your top line was {top task} at {cost}, and I'd hate to leave you with
> nothing: {the specific free thing}. {One sentence on how.}

**Too small / obviously not a fit** (one or two staff, tiny numbers).

> Honest answer: at your size I'd not spend money on this yet. {The one free thing}
> will get you most of the way. Come back when {specific trigger — third employee,
> second system, whatever}.

## What not to do

- Don't attach a PDF. Don't build a PDF generator. Don't promise a document.
- Don't add them to anything. There is no list.
- Don't chase more than once. One follow-up after a week, then leave it:
  > "Nothing needed from you — just closing the loop in case it got buried. If the
  > {top task} thing is still annoying you in a few months, I'm here."
- Don't quote a price in the first reply unless they ask. The page already does that.
